# Pop-in

A staff workspace for independent barbershops in Aotearoa. Today and Calendar support phone bookings, immediate walk-ins, moves, cancellations, completion, no-shows and blocked time. The walk-in queue is available to guests and staff. Dates and times are interpreted in `Pacific/Auckland` and schedule timestamps are stored as UTC instants.

The landing page links to the fictional Rimu demo and to local owner setup. A new owner can create a draft shop, add services, barber profiles, shifts and hours, and make a test booking through the customer page. Setup progress comes from saved records. Test bookings reserve a real calendar slot, are labelled as tests, do not create client profiles and do not receive reminders; cancel the test to reopen its time. The cutover checklist records a manually completed export, an Auckland switchover time and a rollback contact. It does not export data or switch systems automatically.

New shops are private drafts until the owner completes the checklist and publishes locally. The booking link and QR use the current origin; a loopback address works only on this computer. Setup also lets owners create one-use, seven-day staff invitation links. No invitation email is sent: copy the link while it is shown. Accepting it grants shop membership, not owner access, and staff sign-in accounts remain separate from barber profiles. Owner signup, invitation acceptance and publishing are restricted to the local development preview; a real launch needs verified identity, invitation delivery and booking confirmations.

Owners can plan community cut days under Events. Set the event venue, Auckland date and hours, assigned barbers and services, overall capacity and sponsor-funded places. This flow books free sponsored cuts only; a guest can book while both event capacity and a funded place remain. The owner supplies and reviews the English and te reo Māori wording before publishing. Public events appear on the shop booking page; private events need their booking link. Private booking links and separate partner links are shown once when created, and creating a replacement invalidates the old link. Partner links show aggregate place counts only, with no customer details. Links are shared manually and no sponsor payments are processed.

Community bookings use the normal shared barber capacity, including conflicts with linked locations, but event hours apply even when the home shop is closed. Only the booked cut blocks the calendar; add separate blocks for travel or other time at the venue. Cancelling releases a funded place, while a no-show remains allocated. After a booking, the venue, event time, staff and services are fixed; capacity and funded places can still change if they remain at least the allocated count. Saving edits returns the event to draft until the reviewed wording is published again. Guests receive a local confirmation preview and a private cancellation link; to change a community booking they must contact the shop. Community reminders are suppressed until event-specific delivery is available.

The Rotorua directory is at http://127.0.0.1:3001/directory. Only published Rotorua shops that an owner has opted in under Settings → Directory are listed. Owners can add a description and up to five portfolio photos; opting out immediately hides the listing and its directory photos. The fictional Rimu seed opts in once, without overriding a later choice. Service names and morning, afternoon or evening filters use live booking-engine availability; results show the next available time and can change before confirmation. A directory selection carries the shop, service, barber and time into the existing guest flow. The owner can view individual bookings attributed to those selections under Reports → Directory referrals. Each selection is counted once, including completed test-deposit bookings; cancelled bookings remain visible.

## Calendar integration

Shop owners can open Settings → Integrations and connect one primary calendar per barber. Each sync sends only a generic “Pop-in booking” and its time, and imports external busy time without event titles or customer details. A staged salon appointment is exported as the full appointment window for its booking stylist; secondary stylists, manual blocks and time off are not exported. Busy time uses the shared booking capacity, including linked locations. If it overlaps an existing appointment, that booking is retained, the remaining time stays unavailable, and the owner sees a conflict count. A failed sync keeps the last imported busy time. Disconnect removes imported blocks but leaves previously copied events in the external calendar. Sync now runs immediately; `npm run calendar:worker` runs a separate worker every minute. The sync window is the past seven days and the next 366 days, in Pacific/Auckland; previously exported bookings are still updated if moved beyond that window.

Local development includes a fictional calendar sandbox under the same screen. Its events stay in the local database; it does not connect to Google. To configure the Google provider, use a Google OAuth test project and authorised test account, set `POPIN_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, `POPIN_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `POPIN_GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` and a stable, private 32-byte base64 `POPIN_CALENDAR_TOKEN_KEY`, and register `<origin>/api/integrations/google/callback` as the redirect URI. Restart the app and worker after changing these settings. Google asks for permission to read and change calendar events; the token is encrypted at rest. The Google provider has not yet been exercised against an actual Google test account. Do not use it for live shop scheduling until that provider test and operational setup are complete.

The public Rimu booking page is at http://127.0.0.1:3001/book/rimu. Guests can choose a service, barber or first available, time and contact details without an account. The confirmation includes a private link for moving or cancelling. The link is a bearer secret: anyone who has it can manage that booking. Only its hash is stored, and the secret is carried in the URL fragment rather than the server request path.

Customer messaging is off by default. Confirmation remains a preview until a provider and worker are explicitly configured; guests must save the private link shown on the confirmation screen. Guest and whānau test bookings are available locally. Remote booking creation remains gated. Booking-email events can be tested locally with `POPIN_BOOKING_EMAIL_TEST=true`, a fictional `@example.test` recipient and a 32-byte base64 `POPIN_BOOKING_EMAIL_TEST_KEY`; run `scripts/verify-booking-email-outbox.ts` against the isolated QA database and app. The test provider is simulated and sends nothing. When explicitly enabled, the Node service schedules consented email reminders and processes the encrypted outbox. The pilot requires a sender, provider key, encryption key, HTTPS origin and an explicit recipient allowlist. Only addresses on that list can receive booking emails. Accepted outbox messages are not proof of inbox delivery. Uncertain sends beyond 23 hours require manual review rather than automatic retry.

The Railway preview web service has been retired and no longer deploys from `main`. Its database was left in place. Run QA only on localhost against a separate, seeded fictional database; do not use the preserved preview or production databases. Use mocked or provider test modes for email, SMS and payments.

For the local suite, start a fresh PGlite database on `127.0.0.1:56441`, set `DATABASE_URL=postgresql://postgres:postgres@127.0.0.1:56441/postgres`, `POPIN_ENVIRONMENT=local`, and a fictional `DEMO_PASSWORD`, then run `node scripts/setup.mjs --seed`. Start the app with `node scripts/qa-local-dev.mjs` in another terminal; it binds to `127.0.0.1:3003` and removes live provider credentials. Run `node scripts/qa-suite.mjs`; results and logs are written under `artifacts/qa-20261121`. The runner rejects remote database addresses. If those ports are occupied, set `QA_DB_PORT` and `QA_APP_PORT` to unused loopback ports in both terminals, and point `DATABASE_URL` at the chosen database port.

Whānau booking is available from the shop page for two to four people. It offers one barber back-to-back, or overlapping appointments with different barbers when available. Each person has a calendar appointment linked to one visit. The organiser's email and optional mobile belong to the visit, rather than being copied into each person's client profile. The mobile can receive a single local SMS reminder preview. The organiser's private link moves or cancels every appointment together; staff can also cancel the whole visit or complete or mark individual appointments as no-show. If an appointment has already been completed or marked no-show, contact the shop about further changes.

The Rimu queue is at http://127.0.0.1:3001/queue/rimu, and Today has the public link and a QR code. A loopback QR works only on the computer running the app; a deployed public site address is needed for customers' phones. Guests receive a private queue link and can leave from it. Calling someone shows a local text preview to staff; no text is sent. The queue estimate projects the people ahead against service durations, assigned barbers, opening hours, shifts, manually entered barber delays and active appointments or blocks. Estimates are ranges, not promises; staff should update a barber's ready time when they are running late. Seating creates a walk-in appointment and changes the queue status in one database operation.

The future cancellation waitlist is separate from the walk-in queue. Guests join at http://127.0.0.1:3001/waitlist/rimu and must save their private status link; staff can review waiting customers under Waitlist. A future appointment cancellation or move records an opening. Matching offers use the current availability rules and expire after 30 minutes or before the appointment. Offers do not reserve a time: the first successful claim wins. A service requiring a deposit must go through the existing Stripe test-payment flow. The staff page and the private link check for openings when opened; for automatic local checking, run `npm run waitlist:local` in another terminal. No text or email is sent and there is no production messaging integration. Waitlist entry and claim are restricted to local preview.

From a private booking link, **Book this service again** opens the current service and barber in the normal booking flow, using the current price. Staff can also preview a repeat series from New booking; unavailable dates are shown and skipped only after review, and the available dates are rechecked and saved together. Staff-created repeat bookings do not send customer confirmations.

Booking rules are under Settings → Booking rules. Shops can set online minimum notice, booking horizon and change or cancellation windows, with exceptions for individual barbers. Staff can make bookings or changes outside those online windows; for a group past the online change window, staff can cancel the whole visit and rebook it. Service buffers and one-off closures apply to staff, guest, whānau, imported and walk-in bookings. New and moved appointments use the current buffers; existing appointments retain the buffers saved with them. A closure preview lists affected appointments and must be refreshed if the schedule changes before saving. Saving a closure does not cancel appointments or contact customers: staff must handle those separately. A closure for today stops new queue entries while existing private queue links remain accessible.

Settings → Salon can add chairs or other resources and configure an existing service with application, processing and finishing stages. Application uses the booking stylist; later stages can use that stylist or another assigned team member, and processing can use a resource without a stylist. The timeline shows occupied stylist stages and resource rows separately, so another cut can use a stylist during an unattended processing stage. Existing appointments retain their saved stages until moved. Add a new service in Settings → Services and assign it in Team before configuring it; the original Rimu demo services are unchanged.

Consultation and patch-test requirements and their dated results are visible only to signed-in members of the shop. Records are associated with the client and do not follow an appointment reassigned to another client. The flags are reminders for staff; they do not automatically decide whether a treatment is safe or block a booking. Multi-stage services currently require individual scheduled bookings: they are not available for whānau visits, the walk-in queue, Fresha appointment import or test-deposit holds. Ordinary services continue to work in those flows.

Team & locations lets an owner create another private location for the same business. It copies the current service list and hours; the owner must add staff access, assign services, set shifts and complete setup before publishing. Owners at both locations can link the same barber's profiles. Linked profiles share one scheduling identity, so bookings, blocks and salon stages cannot overlap across locations. Location filters show only locations the signed-in person can access. The shop switcher controls the active location for the rest of the staff workspace.

Owners can set a service price and commission rate for each barber at each location. The selected barber's price is shown before guest confirmation and saved with the appointment; moving an existing booking keeps its agreed price. A temporarily unassigned service keeps its configured barber price for later reassignment. Owner-entered timesheets record completed work in Pacific/Auckland and cannot overlap for a linked person. Earnings recognise a completed, fully paid service using the agreed service price and the commission rate at recognition; products, prepaid sales and test payments are excluded. Rates must be set before recognition: earlier sales are not backfilled. Refunds create linked commission corrections, and owners can add a reasoned adjustment to the original sale. Payouts cover the selected period and record money already paid elsewhere; they do not transfer money. Check earlier periods for unpaid amounts and corrections. Only owners can manage prices, rates, timesheets and earnings.

Clients are available in the staff rail. Every new appointment gets a separate client profile unless staff explicitly link an existing profile when creating a booking. Whānau members remain separate even when they share a mobile number; matching contacts are suggestions for staff review, not automatic merges. Staff can reassign an appointment between client profiles without moving the booking. Existing appointments are backfilled as separate profiles because identity cannot safely be inferred from contact details alone. Whānau organiser contact details are not copied to individual participants. Profile contact edits do not change the contact saved on an existing booking. Private notes are visible only to staff with access to that shop. Prices shown in history are booking snapshots where available; older staff bookings may have no recorded price.

Owners can use Campaigns to draft SMS offers, review requests, quiet-day offers and reactivation messages. Existing contacts are not opted in: customers can choose SMS offer preferences from their private booking link, or an owner can record explicit permission and its source in Campaigns → SMS consent. Consent is per shop and mobile number, is separate from booking reminders, and is rechecked when previews run. The quiet-day audience uses current availability; reactivation means a completed visit at least 90 days ago with no upcoming booking. Loyalty campaigns are unavailable until a loyalty programme exists. Campaigns and Inbox use local test messages only; no real SMS or provider delivery receipts are available. Open a recipient's test message to simulate a reply or STOP, and use Inbox to view or answer it. The booking link attributes a matching mobile's booking for 30 days while consent remains active. To run scheduled previews automatically, start `npm run campaigns:local` in another terminal; owners can also run due previews from Campaigns.

Reminders are under Settings → Reminders and are disabled by default. Each shop chooses one channel and lead time. The delivery log stores the actual local preview text or a simulated failure; its SMS count is a count of previews, not sent or billed messages. For automatic local checks, keep `npm run reminders:local` running in another terminal. You can also check due reminders or simulate a failure from the settings page. The worker is restricted to a local database and sends no messages. Reminders are derived from current appointments, so moves and cancellations are reflected on the next check; a group visit produces one organiser reminder. Organisers currently provide email only, so an SMS reminder for a group without a mobile contact is recorded as a failure. All signed-in shop members can manage reminder settings. No production delivery or provider receipts are implemented.

Shop owners can open Reports → Daily close for appointment outcomes, agreed service value and utilisation in Pacific/Auckland, and export the report as CSV. There is no real-money payment integration. Collected money and unpaid balances are unavailable until an owner starts manual tracking and records payments and refunds already handled elsewhere; amounts never entered will be missing. A tender method that was not entered is shown as unavailable. The screen explains which statuses and dates are included, and that utilisation uses current hours and shifts rather than historical snapshots. The tracking start date cannot currently be changed in the app. Reports do not process payments or refunds.

Staff can open checkout from an appointment. After an owner starts manual payment tracking in Reports, staff can record a cash or external EFTPOS deposit or balance already handled outside Pop-in. Previously recorded payments are applied once to the agreed service price. Fees can be entered when known; an unknown fee stays unknown and is not added to the customer's balance. Receipts are printable staff records and are not emailed. Owners can record a refund against a specific original transaction after returning the money through its original tender; Pop-in does not send funds or control a terminal. Historical refunds with no linked original reduce refundable amounts conservatively. Reports show recorded refunds, known fees and whether net takings are available; the accounting CSV contains the corresponding transaction, tender, fee and original-payment references. No accounting sync is configured.

Products tracks stock counts, adjustments and purchase orders. Marking a draft order as ordered records an order placed elsewhere and does not contact the supplier. Receiving it adds stock once. The public shop page lists products for purchase in the shop; it does not take online product orders.

At an appointment's manual checkout, staff can add products or sell gift cards, packages and fixed-visit memberships. Gift card balances are NZD; packages and memberships hold visits for the selected client and service. Memberships have an expiry and do not renew automatically. Prepaid items become usable after the sale is fully recorded. The private code is shown in the issuing browser session and cannot currently be recovered if lost; give it to the customer before closing the session. Customers can check gift-card balances from the shop's products page, and staff redeem them at checkout. An owner can record a whole-sale refund after returning external payments: it restores the sale's product stock and any prepaid redemption together. Prepaid items sold in that sale must be unused before it can be refunded. Pop-in does not move money or send codes. Products and prepaid are unavailable in Stripe test checkout.

An appointment with a Stripe **test** deposit has a clearly marked test checkout. Its test deposit and simulated cash or EFTPOS entries stay separate from the real manual ledger and accounting export. If that appointment already has a non-zero balance in the real manual ledger, an owner must review it in Reports before using test checkout. Test receipts are marked as such. Neither test nor manual checkout processes a real payment.

## Deposits in Stripe test mode

Owners can configure a fixed or percentage deposit per service under Deposits. The customer reviews the price, deposit, remainder and cancellation terms before Stripe Checkout. This is **test mode only**: real Stripe keys are rejected and no real money is taken. Test payments are kept separate from the report's manual payment ledger. The existing confirmation is still a local preview, so customers must save their private link.

To try the provider flow, a Stripe account with Connect enabled is needed. Add `STRIPE_TEST_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_TEST_WEBHOOK_SECRET` to the local `.env`, then restart the app. Use a test key and a webhook signing secret, never a live key. For local events, run the Stripe CLI with `stripe listen --forward-to http://127.0.0.1:3001/api/deposit-webhook` and put its displayed signing secret in `.env`. The endpoint handles Checkout completion, expiry and asynchronous payment events, plus refund updates. Under Deposits, connect the shop's Stripe test account, finish hosted onboarding and refresh its status. `POPIN_PUBLIC_ORIGIN` can override the return origin; in local development it defaults to `http://127.0.0.1:3001`.

Bookings needing a deposit must start at least 36 minutes ahead. A temporary calendar hold expires after 35 minutes and is released when availability is next checked. Only a verified paid webhook confirms the booking. A payment that arrives after the hold is released does not replace another booking; a test refund is requested and an owner can retry pending or failed refunds from Deposits. A cancellation follows the refund rule and notice window shown at booking. Stripe must confirm a refund before the app marks it complete.

If Stripe test setup is incomplete, a service marked as requiring a deposit cannot be booked online. Whānau visits containing such a service are not supported yet: book the people individually or contact the shop. The return page keeps its private link in that browser session; without messaging, closing or clearing the session can lose the link. This test integration has not been validated with a real Stripe account and is not ready for live payments.

## Shop billing in test mode

Settings → Billing is available only to a shop owner. It manages the shop's Pop-in subscription, separately from customer deposits. No plans, prices or subscriptions are invented by the seed. Configure `POPIN_BILLING_PLANS` as a JSON array of objects with `key`, `name`, `priceId`, `amountCents`, `includedSms` and `interval` (`month` or `year`); the commented example in `.env.example` shows the format. The configured NZD price must match an active recurring Stripe test price. Plan changes are offered only when they are also enabled in the Stripe test billing portal. The portal previews and confirms the change, including any proration.

Use `STRIPE_TEST_SECRET_KEY` and a separate `STRIPE_TEST_BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET` in `.env`. Set `STRIPE_TEST_BILLING_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION` to a test portal configuration ID, or leave it unset to use Stripe's default test portal. Enable payment-method updates and the desired plan changes in that portal. For local webhooks, run `stripe listen --forward-to http://127.0.0.1:3001/api/shop-billing-webhook` and add its signing secret to `.env`, then restart the app. Only test keys and test events are accepted. The latest subscription and up to 30 invoices are reconciled from Stripe; the screen shows when it was last checked. A failed renewal links to the provider's payment-method update flow when enabled. The app does not change staff or guest access when a renewal fails.

There is no connected SMS provider, so actual SMS usage is unavailable. Local reminder previews are not counted as sent messages. The configured allowance is shown; the usage counter is designed around provider-recorded units and the subscription's exact start-inclusive, end-exclusive period. The isolated billing test uses clearly marked fictional usage. SMS overage charging is not implemented. This integration has been exercised against a local simulated provider, not a real Stripe account; it is not ready for live billing. Restarting a previously cancelled subscription from Pop-in is not supported yet.

Fresha CSV import is under Settings → Import from Fresha and requires owner access. The fictional `demo@rimu.example` account is granted owner access for Rimu by the seed; local signup grants ownership of the shop being created. Ordinary shop members cannot inspect or import files. There is no owner-management screen yet; for an existing shop without an owner, an administrator must deliberately add an existing staff/shop membership to `shop_owners` in the database.

Upload a UTF-8 CSV up to 2 MB / 5,000 rows, map the columns, resolve barber names and review row conflicts before confirming. Client, service and appointment exports can be imported separately; a mapped Fresha client ID lets a later appointment export refer to the same profile. Without source IDs, duplicate protection covers an identical file and row order only. Imported data is never merged by phone or email and existing client details are not overwritten. Map full names or separate first and last names. Dates must be YYYY-MM-DD or DD/MM/YYYY, with local 24-hour or am/pm times in Pacific/Auckland; combined date/time values must not have an offset. Only mapped names, contacts, service details, barber preferences and future appointments are imported. Unmapped columns such as client notes, addresses, payment data and historical appointments are not imported.

Existing services must match the imported price and duration, and an existing service must already be assigned to the mapped barber. New services used by appointment rows are assigned to the explicitly mapped barber. The preview checks hours, shifts and overlaps, and confirmation rechecks the plan. Rows needing attention are skipped, and each ready row is applied in its own transaction; the result reports successes and failures. Imported appointments generate no confirmations, do not have guest management links and are excluded from the reminder worker. Raw CSV files are not retained on the server; import records and preview hashes support reconciliation and reimport protection.

## Pilot operations

`/operator` is a separate Pop-in operator view. Set `POPIN_OPERATOR_STAFF_ID` to the exact internal ID of an explicitly authorised staff account; shop membership or ownership alone does not grant access. An unset ID fails closed. Admin Central's sign-in is separate and its browser headers are not trusted as Pop-in authentication. Set `POPIN_ENVIRONMENT` to `local`, `staging`, `pilot` or `production`; an unset or unrecognised value displays Unknown. The operator can see redacted operational signals across shops, but no customer names, contacts, message contents or private notes.

Booking telemetry records only fixed outcome codes for staff and guest booking creation or moves, whānau creation or moves, and queue seating. It starts when this version is running and is best effort: missing or stale signals are Unknown, and counts are observed events rather than proof of complete coverage. Reminder failures shown here are local previews, not provider deliveries. There is no delivery-provider or backup-service telemetry, so those statuses remain Unknown. Marking an event reviewed or reopening it records the operator and time; it does not change a booking or retry a message. Support audit records are retained if a shop is removed.

`npm run restore:staging:local` creates a fresh, fictional Rimu PGlite staging database under ignored `.popin-rehearsal`, takes an in-memory archive, restores it independently and checks migrations, records and the booking overlap constraint. It writes redacted evidence to `artifacts/pilot-staging-restore.json` and the staging database only after every check passes. It does not open `DATABASE_URL`, retain a backup archive or test a managed PostgreSQL backup or production recovery. The view shows that evidence only when connected to that staging database with `POPIN_ENVIRONMENT=staging`.

To inspect the isolated local rehearsal, run the command above, then start the staged database in a separate terminal:

```powershell
$pilotStagePath = (Get-Content -Raw artifacts/pilot-staging-restore.json | ConvertFrom-Json).sourceDir
& .\node_modules\.bin\pglite-server.cmd "--db=$pilotStagePath" --port=56440 --host=127.0.0.1 --max-connections=10
```

In another terminal, start the staging app:

```powershell
$env:DATABASE_URL = 'postgresql://postgres:postgres@127.0.0.1:56440/postgres'
$env:POPIN_ENVIRONMENT = 'staging'
$env:POPIN_OPERATOR_STAFF_ID = 'staging-operator'
$env:POPIN_NEXT_DIST_DIR = '.next-pilot-staging'
& .\node_modules\.bin\next.cmd dev -H 127.0.0.1 -p 3002
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:3002/operator and sign in as `operator@staging.example` using the local `DEMO_PASSWORD`. The separate fictional `staff@staging.example` account cannot open operator routes. These accounts and the local rehearsal are not production provisioning.

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 20.9 or newer. From this folder:

```powershell
npm install
Copy-Item .env.example .env
npm run db:local
```

Leave that terminal open. In a second terminal, run:

```powershell
npm run db:seed
npm run dev
```

The development server binds to IPv4 loopback to keep preview confirmations local. Open http://127.0.0.1:3001. Sign in as `demo@rimu.example` with the `DEMO_PASSWORD` from your local `.env` (the example is `popin-demo-change-me`). This account and password are fictional and for local development only. Seeding is repeatable and does not overwrite edits or reset an existing password. The database is stored in `.popin-data` and survives restarts.

`db:local` runs a local PGlite PostgreSQL-compatible server for convenience. It is intended only for development: it has a single underlying connection and is not a production PostgreSQL service. To use PostgreSQL instead, create a database, set `DATABASE_URL` in `.env`, then run `npm run db:seed` and `npm run dev` without starting `db:local`. Run `npm run db:migrate` to apply migrations without demo data.

For an existing checkout, run `npm run db:migrate` once before starting the updated application. The booking engine checks opening hours, shifts, service assignments and duration, booking rules, blocked time and active bookings. Phone bookings and moves offer starts at 15-minute intervals; a walk-in can start at the current minute. PostgreSQL enforces non-overlap for active entries and their reserved buffers with a GiST exclusion constraint, including when requests race. A failed move leaves the existing booking intact. Ambiguous or nonexistent local times at daylight-saving transitions are rejected.

The Rimu seed contains Ari, Jordan and Sam, three services, shop opening hours, weekly shifts and a fictional generated shop photo. Re-running the seed does not replace an existing shop photo or other edits. Team profiles are separate from staff sign-in accounts. Membership controls which shops an account can access; service and barber records are tied to a shop at the database level.

## Verification

With the local database and development server running, `node --env-file=.env --import tsx scripts/verify-team.ts` checks linked-location booking races and direct database conflicts, barber prices, group booking prices, timesheet overlap, sale-linked commission adjustments, payout retries and permissions on desktop and phone. It removes its temporary fictional locations afterwards.

With the local database and development server running, `node --env-file=.env --import tsx scripts/verify-commerce.ts` checks stock and purchase orders, prepaid sales and visit entitlements, a partial redemption and an interrupted refund retry, concurrent stock sales, shop permissions and desktop/phone screens. It removes its temporary fictional shop afterwards.

`node --env-file=.env --import tsx scripts/verify-shop-billing.ts` starts an isolated local app and simulated provider, then checks test checkout reuse, signed renewal failure and recovery, webhook replay, plan and payment portal flows, invoice history, SMS period boundaries, shop access and desktop/phone layouts. No Stripe or SMS service is contacted; temporary records are removed afterwards.

To exercise the deposit webhook without a Stripe account, start a second local dev server with `STRIPE_TEST_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_local_mock`, `STRIPE_TEST_WEBHOOK_SECRET=whsec_local_mock` and `POPIN_NEXT_DIST_DIR=.next-deposit-test` on port 3004. With the regular local server and database running, `node --env-file=.env --import tsx scripts/verify-deposits.ts` uses temporary fictional shops and a simulated provider to check signed webhook replay, expiry, refunds, permissions and desktop/phone screens. This does not contact or validate Stripe-hosted onboarding or Checkout.

With the local database and development server running, `node --env-file=.env --import tsx scripts/verify-daily-report.ts` checks the daily-close totals against an isolated fictional shop, including a deposit, an unpaid cut, a cancellation, refunds, permissions, CSV and desktop/phone layouts. The temporary records are removed afterwards.

The local QA suite includes customer booking, owner and staff flows, whānau, queue, reports, Founder interest and issue reporting. Database-backed verification scripts reject non-loopback databases; run them through `qa-suite.mjs` so they use the isolated QA app and synthetic data. Provider-related checks use mocks or test-only keys, never live credentials. `npm run build` runs the production build and TypeScript check.


This is pilot infrastructure, not an unrestricted public launch. Before a broader rollout, review identity and delivery controls, staff-account recovery, rate limiting and operational security, and use a managed PostgreSQL database with backups.


Pilot enrolment: public shop signup is closed. Set `POPIN_ENROLMENTS_PAUSED=true` to pause even local owner setup without changing existing shops or bookings. This does not pause bookings. Approved-owner registration and verification are available behind the explicit opt-in configuration below; per-shop launch approval is separate. The Report an issue button opens the private support queue; it is not an email address. Local test bookings do not send confirmations, including after a move or cancellation. The restore rehearsal described above is synthetic only; managed backup, retention, access and a real restore procedure must be verified before launch.

### Approved-owner setup (off by default)

The public site still accepts beta interest at `/beta`; the operator reviews those requests in the existing beta queue. To let an approved owner create an account, an operator can explicitly configure `POPIN_OWNER_SIGNUP_ENABLED=true` and add their exact email to the comma-separated `POPIN_OWNER_SIGNUP_ALLOWED_EMAILS`. This also requires an HTTPS `POPIN_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, `RESEND_API_KEY` and `POPIN_EMAIL_FROM`. Missing configuration keeps remote setup closed. `POPIN_ENROLMENTS_PAUSED=true` overrides the enable flag.

When configured, the public beta page links to `/setup`. An approved email can create an unverified owner account and a private shop draft. The owner requests the existing verification email from Setup and follows its one-use link. Approval to register does **not** approve the shop to launch: invitations and publishing still require the separate `POPIN_PILOT_SHOP_ID` decision and a verified owner; publishing and customer booking delivery also require the existing sender, encryption and verified-restore gates. A second shop does not inherit that approval. No configuration is enabled by this code change.

After building with `POPIN_NEXT_DIST_DIR=.next-qa-build`, `scripts/verify-approved-owner-signup.mjs` tests these production-mode gates through a local HTTPS proxy and fake inbox against the isolated QA database. Use a fresh PGlite instance with `--max-connections=100` for the production build’s separate module pools. It requires OpenSSL and Chromium (`CHROME_PATH` if needed), uses ports 3006/3443 by default, blocks external fetches in the test app, and removes its temporary accounts, shops, certificates and mailbox. It checks the default-off UI/API, approved-email registration, private drafts, real verification-page flow and approval isolation across two shops. Never run it against a hosted database.

Verified-owner recovery is separately disabled by default. With migration `060_owner_password_recovery` applied, `POPIN_OWNER_RECOVERY_ENABLED=true` plus the existing HTTPS origin and transactional sender exposes **Recover owner account** on sign-in. Request responses are generic; unknown, unverified and non-owner accounts send nothing. Tokens are hashed, expire after 30 minutes, supersede prior links and can be used once. Requests are limited to five per account per hour. A successful reset revokes all sessions for that account and preserves shop approval independently. Failed email delivery invalidates the new token. `verify-owner-recovery.ts` covers these rules with fake delivery; the local HTTPS signup check also exercises recovery through the browser and rejects the old password. This does not provide employee-account recovery.
